# Conditions while a controller waits

- A writer that waits for another condition at a new generation leaves its own at the generation it last described; only a wait at the generation it already describes is False, `WaitingFor<X>` ([decision](../../docs/decisions/independent-controllers.md)).
- A refusal leaves availability as it is: a readiness condition keeps the status of what exists and serves, True under the refusal's reason (`Refused`, `ObjectsControlledElsewhere`, `AuthenticationInvalid`, `SecretNotFound`) or False under its own while what it reports does not serve, with the refusal at the end of its message and the generation it describes (`statusapply.Refusing`, `statusapply.Described`), and is the refusal itself while what it reports is missing; a writer whose condition says that every object is applied, Tenant's and PKI's, applies past an object refused and is True under a refusal only while every object refused exists and every other is applied, and Workloads applies past one every object that does not mount a refused ConfigMap ([decision](../../docs/decisions/independent-controllers.md)).
- A renderer held by `status.target.operatorRevision` reports what runs, at the generation its condition describes, as a wait does; Workloads' `WaitingForTarget` replaces a condition at the generation it describes unless that condition reports availability, a refusal over it included, and a wait that tries nothing keeps a condition under a refusal's reason as it is while its count agrees with that condition's status, and on a held tenant any condition it counts that is not True, so that the first pass past it tries, unless the target is one the running operator's table does not hold, which no pass under that revision passes; Workloads' wait for a Ready node at a `nodePort` endpoint is such a wait; Workloads' held wait for `CertificatesReady` or `DatastoreReady` over a condition it counts tries as a held pass does, below, rendering past what it waits for and writing nothing, and reports its count with what the trial finds in place of the refusal kept, or, over a refusal at the object's generation that reports no availability, Workloads' wait for the grant, as a held pass does, while a held wait that cannot build the render, without the Datastore's endpoints, with a Secret not hashed yet, without a Ready node at a `nodePort` endpoint or with a target the table does not hold, tries only the Service and the routes; what a held pass or a held wait finds ends a condition it builds anew, a count, a wait at the object's generation or Workloads' wait for the grant, `WaitingForTarget`, over which a refusal takes its own reason under False, but for a write no dry run can try alone, and never one it keeps as it is; Tenant keeps `AddonsApplied` but for the refusals it meets and for True over a `WaitingFor*` reason; nothing reads a condition's message: a held pass carries no refusal, and while a condition of its writer holds a refusal (`contract.Refusals`, read from the reason) or is not True (read from the status) it sends every write of the grant's first pass by dry run, but the Datastore controller's request to etcd-druid, and runs Lodia's own checks on that render, and reports what it finds with `statusapply.Tried`: a refused dry run under `Refused`, which holds every grant, one a webhook of the management cluster does not support (`apistatus.DryRunUnsupported`, which no tenant's API server answers) under `RefusedBeforeHold`, which Lifecycle grants in the window, every one accepted as at rest, and one without an answer as no status written; a refusal it meets itself, a creation or another owner's object, keeps its own reason and comes first ([decision](../../docs/decisions/upgrades-and-maintenance-windows.md)).
- Every wait of Tenant, held or not, keeps a condition that describes an earlier generation, as the first rule says, and an `AddonsApplied` that is `Refused`, `ObjectsControlledElsewhere` or `Applying`: a lost access undoes no observation, and a held pass writes True over a wait at the generation it names.
- A writer that decides from its own condition or status field while it waits, or keeps one while its calls to the tenant fail, reads it through `internal/live` and compares its status apply with that copy, not the cache's; its ClusterRole needs `get` on `controlplanes`.
- A writer whose status apply carries the `resourceVersion` it read, and whose watch requeues every update of its object, needs no live read: a pass on a stale cache conflicts, or skips a write that the next pass makes.
- Every status apply goes through `internal/statusapply`: a `ControlPlane`'s reads the object live once it finds a change, so its ClusterRole needs `get` on `controlplanes`, and `Backup`, `KubeconfigRequest`, the Datastore controller's every `ControlPlane` status apply and Lifecycle's write of a `ControlPlane` that moves a minor step's target apply under the `resourceVersion` they read instead, and take the transitions from that copy.
- A writer's first pass, the condition `statusapply.FirstPass` builds, never replaces a condition of its type that the API server holds with another reason: the status apply keeps that one, so a writer may tell its first pass from the cache.
- A writer records no event itself: its `statusapply.Writer`, built at the call from its `Client`, `Reader`, field manager and `Events`, records one per transition of a condition it writes; `SetupWithManager` sets `Events` from `GetEventRecorder("lodia.io/<controller>")`, a reconciler built as a literal records nothing, and its ClusterRole needs `create` and `patch` on `events.k8s.io` `events`.
